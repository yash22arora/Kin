# Kin Privacy Policy

*Last updated: [DATE — set before publishing]*

Kin is built on a simple idea: your relationships are yours. This policy is
short because there isn't much to disclose — that's by design.

## What Kin stores, and where

Everything you put into Kin — the people you name, the moments you log, your
notes, photos, and feelings — is stored **on your device**. Kin has no
accounts, no sign-up, and no server of its own. We cannot read your notes,
see your photos, or know the names of the people in your sky, because none
of that ever reaches us.

If you delete the app, that data is deleted with it. You can export
everything at any time from Settings → Export my sky.

## What Kin never collects

- The names of people in your sky
- The contents of your notes or moments
- Your photos
- Your contacts (Kin never asks for contact access)
- Your location
- Any advertising identifier — Kin contains no ads and no trackers for ads

## Usage analytics

To understand how Kin is used and improve it, we collect a small amount of
**anonymous usage data** through PostHog: which features are used (for
example, "a moment was logged" or "a widget was added"), device type, and
app version. These events never include the content of what you wrote, who
it was about, or anything that identifies you personally. We do not create
user profiles, and this data is not linked to your identity.

## Notifications

If you choose to set a stargazing hour, Kin schedules one local notification
per day on your device. This never involves a server.

## Face ID

If you enable the lock, Face ID is handled entirely by iOS. Kin never sees
your biometric data — it only receives a yes/no from the system.

## Purchases

Kin offers one lifetime purchase, processed entirely by Apple through the
App Store. We do not receive or store your payment details.

## Children

Kin does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone,
children included — it isn't designed to collect personal information at all.

## Changes

If this policy ever changes in a way that matters, the app will tell you
plainly before the change applies.
